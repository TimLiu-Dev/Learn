Rectified Flow, Samplers, Schedulers & Prompting as Specification

Why SDXL needs curved multi-step schedules while FLUX.2 and Wan 2.2 travel straight lines—plus how Samplers, Schedulers, Timestep Shift, and typed prompts work together.

Mission Tie-In
Choosing the wrong sampler, noise schedule, or guidance mode wastes hours on your M5 Max / Z13 and ruins skin realism on LinkedIn and Tinder portraits. Today you master the exact solver mechanics behind late-2026 models.

1. Classic DDPM (SDXL) vs. Rectified Flow (FLUX.2 & Wan 2.2)

Primary Docs: See FlowMatchEulerDiscreteScheduler vs. DPMSolverMultistepScheduler in Diffusers. Think of denoising like navigating from pure Gaussian static (t = 1.0) to a clean portrait (t = 0.0). Classic DDPM / Score-Based Diffusion (SD 1.5, SDXL) follows curved, stochastic paths—so solvers need 20–30 careful steps and rely heavily on negative_prompt + CFG 5.5–7.5.

Modern Transformer architectures—FLUX.1 / FLUX.2, SD 3.5, and Wan 2.1 / 2.2—use Rectified Flow (Flow Matching). Instead of predicting curved noise residuals, the network learns a direct velocity vector pointing in a near-straight line from noise to data: 

# Classic DDPM/SDXL: curved trajectory, needs negative_prompt + CFG ~6.5
# Rectified Flow (FLUX.2 / Wan 2.2): straight velocity field v_theta(z_t, t)
z_next = z_t + (t_next - t_now) * model_velocity(z_t, t_now, prompt_emb)

2. Sampler vs. Scheduler vs. Timestep Shift

In ComfyUI and Diffusers, two separate choices govern how the denoising loop moves from 1.0 → 0.0:

  • Scheduler (The Step Spacing / Sigma Curve): Decides which noise levels (σ) you visit at each step. Classic SDXL pairs well with karras (denser steps near clean fine detail). Flow Matching models expect simple, normal, or beta schedules.
  • Sampler (The ODE/SDE Solver Algorithm): Decides how to step between two noise levels. euler is fast, deterministic, and standard for Flow Matching; dpmpp_2m (2nd-order multistep) excels on SDXL; ancestral/SDE samplers (euler_ancestral, dpmpp_2m_sde) inject fresh noise each step for organic variation, though they converge differently at high step counts.
  • Timestep Shift (ModelSamplingFlux / ModelSamplingSD3): High-resolution 1024px+ or video latents carry more spatial correlation, so Flow Matching pipelines apply a resolution-dependent shift (e.g., shift = 1.15–3.0 for FLUX, shift = 5.0–8.0 for Wan 2.2) that spends more steps sculpting global layout early before sharpening details.
Architecture Recommended Sampler + Scheduler Steps & Guidance Mode
SDXL / Pony dpmpp_2m + karras (or dpmpp_2m_sde + karras) 24–30 steps · True CFG 5.5–7.5 (negative_prompt active)
FLUX.2 [dev] / FLUX.1 [dev] euler + simple or beta 20–28 steps · Distilled guidance 2.5–4.0, True CFG 1.0
FLUX.2 [klein] (Distilled) euler + simple 4–8 steps · Distilled guidance 1.0–3.5
Wan 2.2 A14B (+ LightX2V) euler + simple + ModelSamplingSD3 (shift=8.0) 20 steps standard (or 4–6 steps with LightX2V/Lightning LoRA)

3. Guidance-Distilled vs. Standard CFG (The #1 FLUX Trap)

In SDXL, CFG runs the model twice per step (prompt vs. negative_prompt). In guidance-distilled checkpoints like FLUX.1 [dev] or FLUX.2 [dev], CFG is baked into an embedded guidance conditioning scalar (typically 2.5–4.0) while true CFG stays at 1.0—meaning negative_prompt is ignored unless you force true CFG at double the compute cost!

4. Prompting as a Typed Engineering Spec

Stop writing spam tags like "masterpiece, best quality, 8k uhd, ultra realistic" on modern T5/Mistral/VLM encoders (FLUX.2, Imagen 4, GPT-Image-2.6). That works on SDXL / Pony tag-trained CLIP encoders, whereas modern encoders expect natural photography specs in priority order:

Prompt Spec Template · Natural Language Encoders
[Subject & Trigger] + [Pose & Framing] + [Wardrobe & Texture] + [Lighting & Lens] + [Environment]

# Example (LinkedIn):
"ohwxman, head-and-shoulders studio portrait facing camera with a relaxed,
confident half-smile, wearing a tailored charcoal wool blazer over a crisp
white oxford shirt. Soft diffused 45-degree key light with subtle catchlights
in eyes, 85mm f/2.0 lens, shallow depth of field, neutral warm gray backdrop."
