Training Your Personal LoRAs: AI-Toolkit & Kohya_ss

Turning your 22 selfies and 20 cat photos into portable .safetensors adapters—with VRAM-safe 48GB L40S configs, rank/alpha math, Wan 2.2 MoE dual-expert LoRAs, and checkpoint selection.

1. How LoRA Works & Key Hyperparameters

Instead of retraining or saving a 32GB–64GB base checkpoint, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) freezes base weights and injects compact rank-r update matrices (A × B) into attention layers—yielding a 40MB–300MB .safetensors file you can hot-swap at strength 0.75–1.0.

  • Rank (linear) & Alpha (linear_alpha): Rank 16–32 is ideal for a single person or cat. Setting alpha = rank (scale 1.0) or alpha = rank / 2 (gentler updates) keeps learning stable without capacity bloat.
  • Caption Dropout (0.05–0.10): Randomly drops caption tokens on ~5–10% of steps so your trigger word learns robustly even with varied prompts.

2. Model & VRAM Reality Check (48GB L40S vs. 80GB H100)

Base Target 48GB L40S Fit & Trainer Critical Gotcha
FLUX.2-klein-base-9B / 4B Flies on 48GB L40S (ai-toolkit) Always train on the undistilled base checkpoint—never the 4-step distilled inference variant!
FLUX.1-dev (12B) Fits 24GB–48GB with quantize: true (ai-toolkit) Huge ecosystem of compatible ControlNets, PuLID, and realism LoRAs.
FLUX.2-dev (32B + 24B Mistral-3) 76GB+ BF16 → use 80GB H100 (or heavy quantization + text-encoder caching) Trying unquantized BF16 FLUX.2-dev training on a 48GB L40S triggers immediate CUDA OOM.
Wan 2.2 A14B (MoE Video) 48GB L40S / 80GB H100 (ai-toolkit) Wan 2.2 A14B uses separate High-Noise and Low-Noise experts—producing a matched _high_noise + _low_noise LoRA pair.
SDXL / Pony / Illustrious 24GB–48GB (kohya_ss / sd-scripts) TOML/UI config; supports granular UNet vs. CLIP Text Encoder LR + AdamW8bit.
YAML · Complete 48GB L40S ai-toolkit Config (Personal Identity / Cat LoRA)
job: extension
config:
  name: "my_face_flux2_klein_v1"
  process:
    - type: "sd_trainer"
      training_folder: "/workspace/output"
      device: "cuda:0"
      trigger_word: "ohwxman"
      network:
        type: "lora"
        linear: 16
        linear_alpha: 16
      save:
        save_every: 250
        max_step_saves_to_keep: 8
      datasets:
        - folder_path: "/workspace/datasets/me_raw"
          caption_ext: "txt"
          caption_dropout_rate: 0.05
          cache_latents_to_disk: true
          resolution: [512, 768, 1024]
      train:
        batch_size: 1
        steps: 2000
        optimizer: "adamw8bit"
        lr: 1e-4
        dtype: "bf16"
        noise_scheduler: "flowmatch"
      model:
        name_or_path: "black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-base-9B"
        arch: "flux2"
        quantize: true         # on-the-fly qfloat8 keeps 48GB L40S fast with huge headroom
        low_vram: false        # L40S 48GB holds 9B cleanly without CPU offload!
      sample:
        sample_every: 250
        width: 1024
        height: 1024
        prompts:
          - "ohwxman, studio LinkedIn headshot, charcoal blazer, softbox light"
          - "ohwxman, full body walking in sunny park, casual denim jacket"

3. Evaluating Checkpoints: Overfitting vs. Underfitting

  • Underfitting (e.g., Step 500): General vibe matches, but eye spacing or cat fur markings look like a cousin, not you.
  • Sweet Spot (often Step 1250–1750): Instant recognition AND the model still obeys new outfits, lighting, and camera angles cleanly.
  • Overfitting ("Fried / Stiff", e.g., Step 2500+): Face looks hyper-contrasty or skin turns waxy, and prompts like "at a beach" keep pasting your indoor lighting. Pick the earlier _000001500.safetensors checkpoint or dial LoRA strength in ComfyUI down to 0.75–0.85!
