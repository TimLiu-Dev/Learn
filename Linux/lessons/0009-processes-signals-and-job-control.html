1. Android Developer → Linux Mental Bridge

Transferring Prior Knowledge

On Android, Zygote forks app processes and the OS sends signals when backgrounding or killing apps. On Linux servers, every running program is forked from a parent, tracked under /proc/<pid>, and controlled via POSIX signals.

2. Reading ps aux, top & Understanding Zombies (Z)

Process States

Processes exist in states like R (running/runnable), S (interruptible sleep waiting on I/O), D (uninterruptible disk sleep—cannot even be killed with SIGKILL!), and Z (zombie: already dead, waiting for its parent to read its exit code via wait()). You cannot kill -9 a zombie because it is already dead—you must fix or kill its parent!

example-01: Inspecting Running Processes & Signals 
ps aux    # Snapshot of all processes across users
top       # Interactive CPU/memory view
kill -l   # List available POSIX signals

3. Graceful Shutdowns (SIGTERM) & nice / nohup

Signals & Job Control

kill <pid> sends SIGTERM (15) by default, giving the app a chance to close DB connections cleanly. Only escalate to kill -9 <pid> (SIGKILL) if the process hangs or ignores SIGTERM. Use nohup cmd & when you must ignore SIGHUP outside tmux.

example-02: Adjusting Priority & Terminating a Runaway Worker 
renice -n 10 -p 4821    # Lower CPU priority of heavy worker
pkill -f gradle-worker   # Signal processes matching pattern

Real Outage Pattern: Why kill -9 Left Stale Locks & Uncommitted WAL Files

An engineer restarted a stuck Postgres container via immediate kill -9 rather than waiting 10 seconds for SIGTERM checkpointing. Recovery replayed gigabytes of WAL logs on startup, turning a 5-second graceful restart into a 12-minute outage.

Interview Q1

Why can't you kill a process in state `D` or `Z` with `kill -9`?

Model Answer: A Z (zombie) is already dead waiting for its parent to call wait(). A D process is sleeping uninterruptibly inside a kernel I/O syscall and won't process pending signals until that kernel call returns.

Interview Q2

What happens to orphan child processes if their parent dies before calling `wait()`?

Model Answer: The kernel re-parents orphans to PID 1 (systemd) or the nearest subreaper, which automatically calls wait() when they exit.
