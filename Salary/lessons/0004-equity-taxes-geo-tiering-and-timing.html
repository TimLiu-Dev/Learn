Fine-Print Cash & Tax Traps: 22% RSU Withholding, ISO AMT, Geo-Tiers & Timing

Engineers moving from $90k cash into $200k–$700k+ equity offers regularly get ambushed by surprise April tax bills, 90-day option "golden handcuffs", or silent 15% remote pay cuts. Know these mechanics before you sign.

Skill Unlocked: Withholding Gap Math, PTEW Buy-In Sizing & Geo-Tier Scaling

Payoff: Avoid surprise IRS tax bills and illiquid option traps

1. The 22% Supplemental RSU Withholding Trap

Under IRS Publication 15, employers typically withhold a flat 22% federal income tax on supplemental wages—which includes RSU vests, cash bonuses, and sign-on cash—up to $1M/yr (37% above $1M). When your total income puts you in the 32% or 35% marginal federal bracket, that 10%–13% gap creates a massive April tax bill unless you sell vested RSUs and set aside cash or adjust W-4 withholding.

RSU Concentration Rule
Public RSUs are taxed as ordinary W-2 income on the exact day they vest—identical to receiving a cash bonus and immediately buying company stock. Selling at vest (or setting up automated diversification) prevents owing taxes on shares that later drop 40%.

2. Startup Golden Handcuffs: 90-Day PTEW, ISO AMT & 83(b)

  • 90-Day Post-Termination Exercise Window (PTEW): As Carta's stock option guides explain, most startup ISO plans give you just 90 days after leaving to pay cash (`Vested Shares × Strike Price`) to keep your vested options—or forfeit them forever. If a startup offers a 7–10 year PTEW, note that ISOs legally convert to NSOs 90 days after departure.
  • ISO Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT): Exercising ISOs triggers no regular income tax, but the paper spread (409A FMV − Strike Price) counts toward AMT. You can owe tens of thousands in IRS cash taxes on illiquid startup paper you cannot sell.
  • 83(b) Election (30-Day Clock): Only applies if a startup allows early exercise of unvested options into restricted stock—filing an 83(b) within 30 days locks in tax at the low early valuation, not Standard unexercised options.

3. Geographic Pay Tiers & Start-Date Timing

  • Geo-Tier Multipliers (Levels.fyi): Most Tier 2/3 firms tie pay to local cost-of-labor: Zone A (SF/NYC/Seattle = 1.00×), Zone B (Boston/LA/Austin/Chicago = ~0.90–0.95×), and Zone C / National Remote (Midwest/Remote = ~0.75–0.90×). Moving from SF to Remote later can trigger a 10%–20% base/refresher cut!
  • Start-Date Alignment: Align your start date to clear your current employer's annual bonus (e.g., JHT's $5,000 bonus) and avoid missing your new employer's quarterly RSU vest cutoff by 3 days.
