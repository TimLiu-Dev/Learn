Why it works. The two headers use two date formats "for historical reasons", as RFC 9745 §4 puts it: Deprecation is a Structured Field Date, @ plus Unix seconds, and Sunset is an HTTP-date, which senders "MUST generate" as an IMF-fixdate (RFC 9110 §5.6.7). One formatter with a fixed locale and zone gives the same bytes on every machine, and require enforces RFC 9745's rule that the sunset "MUST NOT be earlier" than the deprecation.

Common mistake. DateTimeFormatter.RFC_1123_DATE_TIME sounds exactly right, and prints Sun, 5 Mar 2028: its day of month has one or two digits. It passes every test written on the 10th or later, which is why the second test uses the 5th. The other classic slip is a formatter without Locale.ENGLISH, which follows the JVM's default locale.
