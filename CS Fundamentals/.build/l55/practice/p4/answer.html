Why it works. The order mirrors the two questions: does the thing exist, who are you (401), and are you allowed (403). Each 200 branch names exactly what one kind of person may do, and else -> 403 catches the rest. So the admin can't edit, nobody can SHARE, and the next action someone adds starts out denied: OWASP's deny by default and least privilege.

Common mistake. Writing the rule the other way round: "a stranger who isn't an admin gets 403, everyone else gets 200". It looks equivalent, but it lets the admin edit and the owner SHARE, because nothing said no.
