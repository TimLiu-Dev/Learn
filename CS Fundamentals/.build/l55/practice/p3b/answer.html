Why it works. The browser, not the page, writes origin into the client data, and the signature covers a hash of those exact bytes. So a login signed on a phishing page carries the phishing origin, and editing it afterwards breaks the signature: either way one of your checks fails. The server needed no secret for any of it, only the public key from registration.

Common mistake. Matching the origin loosely, for example origin.endsWith("books.example"). https://evilbooks.example ends with that too. WebAuthn §13.4.9: a site served only at one origin "SHOULD require origin to exactly equal" it; with several, compare against an exact list.
