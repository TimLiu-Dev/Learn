Why it works. Lamport's two rules are the whole algorithm: count every event (IR1), and on a receive jump past both your own clock and the message's stamp (IR2). So every edge of happened-before, program order or a message, goes to a bigger number. A vector keeps one count per process, so it can say "neither saw the other", which one Lamport number can't.

Common mistake. A receive of stamp + 1 that forgets maxOf with the receiver's own clock. When P2 is already at 4 and a message stamped 1 arrives, its clock falls back to 2, below its own earlier events. A second trap is comparing vectors by their sums: [2,1,0] and [1,1,3] would look "before" when they're concurrent.
