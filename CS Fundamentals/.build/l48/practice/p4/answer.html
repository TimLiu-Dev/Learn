Why it works. add only ever turns bits on, so an added item's three bits stay set, and the filter can never say "no" to something it holds. An absent item gets "maybe" when other items happen to have set all three of its bits. With 16 items in 64 bits, that's common, and Part 2's Bayes calculation shows how many of those "maybe"s are wrong once present keys are rare.

Common mistake. (1 shl i).toLong(): the shift happens in Int, so positions 32 to 63 land on the wrong bit, and position 31 gives a negative Int whose sign fills all 32 high bits when it's widened. Shift a 1L instead.
