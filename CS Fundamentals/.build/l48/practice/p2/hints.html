Bayes' rule: P(outage | alert) = P(alert | outage) · P(outage) / P(alert). Two of those three numbers are parameters. Which one isn't?

---

P(alert) adds up both ways an alert can happen: during an outage (hitRate * prior) and during a normal minute (falseAlarmRate * (1 - prior)). For stdDev, compute the mean, then the average of (x - mean)², then take sqrt.

---

Return hitRate * prior / pAlert. If the "ignores the system" check fails, look at it on paper: with hitRate == falseAlarmRate, P(alert) is just that rate, so the answer is the prior. In Java, timings.stream().mapToDouble(Double::doubleValue).average() gives an OptionalDouble; Kotlin's average() returns a plain Double.
