Why it works. The denominator is P(alert), split by the law of total probability into "alert during an outage" plus "alert during a normal minute". When outages are rare, the second part dominates, which is why the 1-in-10,000 case gives under 1%. When the alert fires at the same rate either way, it's independent of outages, and Bayes' rule gives back the prior unchanged: the alert taught you nothing. stdDev measures distance from the mean, so shifting every timing by 100 ms doesn't change it.

Common mistake. hitRate / (hitRate + falseAlarmRate), which ignores the prior: the base-rate fallacy. It answers 0.99 for the rare-outage case, where the truth is about 0.01.
