Why it works. A hunk is the old lines and the new lines interleaved: context lines belong to both files, - lines only to the old one, + lines only to the new one. Counting each side separately gives the two lengths in the header, and that's how you check a hunk by eye in a review. The ,1 rule is just Git's shorthand.

Common mistake. Counting "everything that isn't +" as old. That also counts the \ No newline at end of file marker, which isn't a line of either file, so the header comes out one too big.
