Why it works. The job's result is the logical AND of every step that ran, so one boolean that can only go from true to false is enough. Steps are skipped by default after a failure because each step implicitly has GitHub's "default status check of success()"; always opts out of that, which is how jobs still upload test reports from a red build. Catching the exception keeps one broken step from taking down the whole runner, the same way ./gradlew turns a failed test into exit code 1 instead of a crash.

Common mistake. Writing passed = (step succeeded) on every step. Then a green always step at the end turns the whole job green and hides the failed tests: CI would show a green check on a broken build.
