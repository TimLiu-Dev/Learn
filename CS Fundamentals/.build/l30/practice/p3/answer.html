Why it works. The decoder never assumes a chunk lines up with a message. Bytes wait in pending until a whole frame is there, so one read with two messages and three reads with one message give the same result. That's the "every way of cutting" check: all 30 chunk sizes, including cuts through the length prefix and through the two bytes of é. readFully does the same waiting for you, by blocking.

Common mistake. Decoding each chunk on its own, as if one write arrived as one read. It passes when a chunk happens to hold whole frames and silently drops the rest. A second one: putInt(message.length), which counts characters, so héllo claims 5 bytes and the next frame starts one byte early.
