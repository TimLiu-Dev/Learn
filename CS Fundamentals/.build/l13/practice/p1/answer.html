Why it works. The upper bound T : Comparable<T> is a promise the compiler checks at every use: Span(1, 5) and Span("b", "d") compile, while Span(Point(0, 0), Point(1, 1)) wouldn't. Inside the class, that promise is what makes <= legal on T, which Kotlin turns into compareTo calls.

Common mistake. Writing require(lo < hi). It looks the same, but it rejects Span(3, 3), and spanOf(listOf(7)) then throws instead of returning 7..7. A closed range needs <=.
