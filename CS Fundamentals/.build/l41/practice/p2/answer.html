Why it works. putIfAbsent makes "is anyone loading this key?" and "I am" one atomic step (L27), so exactly one thread runs the loader and the rest join() its future (L28). Only that key's callers wait. Removing the entry in finally means a failure is delivered to the current waiters but never sticks: the next get starts a fresh load.

Common mistake. Making get @Synchronized (or synchronized in Java). It does stop the stampede, but now a 20 ms query for one book makes every other book wait in line, cached or not. That's the same "lock held during slow work" problem as computeIfAbsent's warning.
