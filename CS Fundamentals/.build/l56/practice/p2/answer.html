Why it works. Two checks decide whether a record is whole, the same two LogKV makes. The terminator says the writer finished it: here that's the newline, written last; in LogKV it's the length fields. The CRC says the bytes are the ones that were written. The scan stops at the first failure, because after a crash only a prefix of the log is trustworthy, and validLength is exactly where the next append must go.

Common mistake. Splitting the log with split("\n") or lines(). A crash just before the final newline leaves a line whose CRC matches, so it gets recovered, and validLength then points past a record with no newline: the next append is glued onto it. Unpadded hex (toString(16)) is the other: about one CRC in 16 starts with a zero digit, so those records would all look corrupt.
