For each function, ask yourself: is the question "same object?" (identity) or "same contents?" (equality)? Two of the three are about contents.

---

Arrays don't override equals, so == on two arrays only checks the arrows. They have their own content check. A change shows through both a and b only when they're one object, and Kotlin spells that question ===.

---

The three fixes are a.contentEquals(b), a === b and it == typed. Java swaps the symbols: Arrays.equals(a, b), a == b and names.get(i).equals(typed).
