Why it works. After the fix, no reference to the book's list ever leaves the class. The constructor copies the caller's list into one only the book holds, and all hands out a new snapshot each time. A List<Int> type alone only hides add from your reference. The object behind it is still the book's MutableList, which is why the cast in the last check could change it. Java's List.copyOf goes one step further: the snapshot itself refuses writes.

Common mistake. Copying on the way in but returning scores on the way out, trusting the read-only type. Callers then hold a live view: it changes under them when the book changes, and a cast lets them change the book.
