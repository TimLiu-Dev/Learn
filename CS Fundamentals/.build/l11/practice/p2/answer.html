Why it works. This is boundary-value testing: off-by-one bugs (> instead of >=, 1..100 instead of 0..100) live at edges, so the sharpest rows sit right on and right next to them. Running tests against deliberately broken copies is called mutation testing. Tools such as PIT make the mutants for you and report the ones your suite misses.

Common mistake. Picking typical values, one from the middle of each band. Every row is correct and the suite is green, yet it catches none of the six bugs. Green tests only prove what they actually check.
