Why it works. Self time is "time this span spent not waiting on a child". Sorting the children and sweeping counts every covered millisecond once, so overlapping calls don't eat the parent's own time twice, and gaps between children stay the parent's. The root always has the longest duration, but the span with the largest self time is where the work, or the waiting on something untraced, actually happened.

Common mistake. Subtracting the sum of the children's durations, as the Part 3 demo does. It's right for a sequential chain, but a gateway that calls two services in parallel gets 0 or even a negative self time.
