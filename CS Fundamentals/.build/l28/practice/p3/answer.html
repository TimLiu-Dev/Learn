Why it works. A semaphore is a counter of permits: acquire() takes one or waits, release() returns one. With release() in finally, every path out of the block returns its permit, so at most 3 callers are ever inside. On a virtual thread, waiting in acquire() parks it and frees the carrier, which is why JEP 444 recommends semaphores over pooling.

Common mistake. release() as a plain line after block(). Every exception leaks a permit, and the gate slowly closes: the service hangs hours later with no error at all.
