Why it works. Amdahl's denominator is the 1-thread time split in two: the serial part 1 − p stays the same however many cores you add, and only p is divided by n. Little's law multiplies how often requests arrive by how long each one holds a thread. Rounding up matters because you can't run half a thread: 1.5 busy on average means 2, and more for bursts.

Common mistake. requestsPerSecond * latencyMs / 1000 in Ints. It rounds 1.5 threads down to 1, so the pool is undersized, and 50,000 × 60,000 silently overflows to a negative number.
