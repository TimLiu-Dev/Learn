Three rules, three keywords: NOT NULL and UNIQUE on members.email, and REFERENCES members(id) on fines.member_id (plus NOT NULL: a fine always has a member). Then fill members first, because fines needs its ids.

---

INSERT INTO members (name, email) SELECT DISTINCT member_name, member_email FROM fines_flat; gives one row per member. Without DISTINCT, your new UNIQUE constraint stops the insert: that's the constraint doing its job.

---

The fines don't know their new member ids yet, so join to find them: INSERT INTO fines (id, member_id, amount_cents, reason) SELECT f.fine_id, m.id, … FROM fines_flat f JOIN members m ON m.email = f.member_email; Join on the email, not the name: the email is the natural key that UNIQUE protects.
