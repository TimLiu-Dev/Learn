Why it works. A CALL saves exactly one thing, where to come back to, on top of the stack, and RET consumes exactly one. Because the last address pushed is the first popped, nested calls unwind in reverse order, which is the "last in, first out" of L4's call stack. The limit check turns runaway recursion into a clear error, as the JVM's StackOverflowError does.

Common mistake. Pushing at, the CALL's own address, instead of pc. RET then lands on the CALL, which calls again, so the program never gets past it; the tests' step budget reports no HLT after 1000 steps.
