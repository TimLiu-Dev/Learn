For isMinHeap, it's easier to look upwards: every index i from 1 on has one parent, (i - 1) / 2. In siftDown, the children of p are 2 * p + 1 and 2 * p + 2, but only those below n exist.

---

siftDown is the second half of the lesson's poll: pick the smaller child, stop if a[p] <= a[child], otherwise swap and continue from the child. The last parent in an array of size n is at n / 2 - 1.

---

for (i in a.size / 2 - 1 downTo 0) siftDown(a, i, a.size). The order matters: going from the bottom up, both subtrees of i are already heaps when you sift it. Ties: take the right child only if it's strictly smaller, as the JDK does, or the random test will disagree on duplicates. Java's loop is for (int i = a.length / 2 - 1; i >= 0; i--).
