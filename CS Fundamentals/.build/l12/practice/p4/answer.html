Why it works. A fast-forward is possible exactly when your tip is already in their history: nothing on your side needs combining, so the pointer slides forward. If their tip is in your history, there's nothing to bring in. Only when neither contains the other do you need a commit with two parents. Real Git also finds the merge base, the best common ancestor, to run the three-way merge of the files. Here that's c1, the commit both lines grew from.

Common mistake. Following only the first parent in isAncestor. Ordinary commits pass, but after a merge, fix's commit sits behind the merge commit's second parent, so merging back makes a needless extra merge commit instead of a fast-forward.
