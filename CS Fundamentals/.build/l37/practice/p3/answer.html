Why it works. Callers now say what they want (place, next), and the map, the queue type and the "no duplicates" rule are private decisions. You could switch to a database tomorrow and no caller would change. The empty-line cases are defined out of existence: next returns null, and cancel of someone who isn't waiting does nothing. The reflection check pins the interface, just like LoanDeskTest.

Common mistake. Making the field private but returning it from waiting. Kotlin's List type is read-only, not immutable: the object is still the real MutableList, so a caller can cast it and change your queue. Hiding information means hiding the data, not just the field.
