Interview Patterns Roadmap

26 sessions, one pattern each, easiest to hardest. The order follows the NeetCode roadmap, and the problems come from NeetCode 150 and Blind 75.

How to read this. Each session lists one walkthrough problem that we solve together, then 2–3 problems you solve on your own. Click any problem to open it on LeetCode, which supports Kotlin. The High markers show how often a pattern tends to come up in interviews. The method behind them is at the bottom.

How every session runs

  1. Warm-up from memory (10 min). You re-solve one problem from an earlier pattern. Each pattern comes back about 1, 3, and 7 sessions after you first learn it. Spacing reviews out like this builds long-term memory.
  2. The idea. A short lesson page explains the pattern and when to reach for it.
  3. Walkthrough. I solve one problem in interview style: clarify the problem, try examples, describe the approach, write the code, test it, then state the complexity.
  4. Your turn, 2–3 problems. For each one, you first explain your approach out loud as you would to an interviewer. Then you code it in Kotlin and state its time and space complexity. I review it for correctness, edge cases, and cleaner idioms.
  5. Hints come in steps when you're stuck: a nudge, then a direction, then the key insight, then pseudocode. You only see a full solution if you ask for it.

Phase 6 — Mock interviews

Each mock is one problem you haven't seen, chosen from the leftover NeetCode 150 problems or a similar list. You get 35–45 minutes on a timer. I play the interviewer: I answer clarifying questions, push back on your approach, and ask "can we do better?". At the end you get a written scorecard covering four things:

AxisWhat I look for
Problem solvingYou clarify the problem, explore examples, compare more than one approach, and know the best possible complexity.
CodeYour code is correct, clean, and idiomatic Kotlin, with sensible names.
VerificationYou trace through your examples by hand, catch edge cases, and fix bugs without being prompted.
CommunicationYou think out loud, explain tradeoffs, and handle hints well.

For practice with a real person, see the peer-mock options in RESOURCES.md.

How the frequency markers were assigned

No public, authoritative dataset tracks interview frequency by topic. The best data, LeetCode's company tags, is behind a paywall and depends heavily on the company. So the markers combine two expert signals:

  1. Tech Interview Handbook priority tiers from Yangshun Tay, the author of Blind 75. High priority: Array, String, Sorting & searching, Matrix, Tree, Graph. Mid: Hash table, Recursion, Linked list, Queue, Stack, Heap, Trie, Interval. Low: DP, Binary, Math, Geometry. source
  2. Each pattern's share of Blind 75. The list was curated as the most essential problems. Trees (11), 1-D DP (10), and Arrays & Hashing (8) have the most problems. source data

Where the sources disagree: dynamic programming. The handbook rates DP as low priority, but 12 of the Blind 75 problems are DP. In practice it depends on the company: some companies are known for asking DP and many rarely do. I rated it Medium. If you tell me which companies you're targeting, I'll adjust.
