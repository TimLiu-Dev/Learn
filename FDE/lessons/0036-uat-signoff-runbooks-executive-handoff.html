Track 2 · Phase 9 · Unit 0036 · Senior Delivery Craft & Executive Diplomacy

UAT Sign-Off, Runbooks, Pushback & Core Handoff

Operating like an engineer-diplomat when pilots go live: running structured User Acceptance Testing (UAT), pushing back with evidence against risky executive shortcuts, and handing off clean runbooks.

Estimated Time: 25 min Prerequisites: 0002, 0029 Tangible Win: Build a UAT Go-Live Gate & Incident Severity Runbook Router

1. Shipping Is Only Half the Battle — Sustaining Adoption Is the Other Half

A Senior FDE does not declare victory when docker push succeeds. As outlined in roadmap.sh/forward-deployed-engineer and Reflections on Palantir, senior field engineers own three critical closing disciplines:

  1. Structured User Acceptance Testing (UAT): Put 5–10 real customer operators through scripted + unscripted edge cases on production data; require zero open P0/security blockers, verified operator acceptance rates, and explicit sponsor sign-off before turning off legacy shadow workflows.
  2. Diplomatic Executive Pushback: When a buyer says "Skip the evals and push straight to all 10,000 staff tomorrow!", never say plain "no"—offer a quantified option matrix: e.g., "Option A: 50-seat canary tomorrow with rollback guard; Option B: full rollout Friday after CI red-team clears."
  3. Runbooks, Incident Severity & Clean Handoff: Document alert playbooks, rollback flags, SCIM contact owners, and upstreamed platform features so the engagement doesn't require permanent FDE babysitting!
Blameless Postmortem Structure:
Timeline → Root Cause (systemic, not "Joe forgot") → Customer Impact → Immediate Mitigation → Permanent Eval/Code Guard!
