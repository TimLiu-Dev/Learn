Structure-Aware Chunking & Contextual Retrieval

Why naive 500-character fixed-window slicing destroys enterprise tables and orphan pronouns—and how structure-aware chunking plus Anthropic-style Contextual Retrieval slash retrieval failures.

Estimated Time: 25 min Prerequisites: 0005 Tangible Win: Build a Table-Safe Markdown Chunker & Contextual Enricher

1. Why Naive Fixed-Size RAG Fails on Real Enterprise Docs

Tutorial RAG scripts blindly split PDFs every 500 characters. On real corporate policies, engineering SOPs, and financial filings, fixed slicing creates two fatal failures:

  1. Orphaned Context: Chunk 14 reads "Revenue rose 14% YoY under this exemption"—without stating which subsidiary, fiscal year, or product line it belongs to!
  2. Shattered Markdown / Pricing Tables: Splitting halfway through a table strips column headers from row numbers, causing the LLM to invent or misread SLA limits.

As shown in Anthropic's Contextual Retrieval research, prepending a concise chunk-level context header (identifying document, section hierarchy, and entity) before indexing both BM25 and embeddings cuts top-20 retrieval failures dramatically.

Provenance Matters:
Always store parentDocId, sectionPath, and page/line metadata so the UI can highlight the exact source block—never ungrounded mystery paragraphs.

2. Annotated Code: Contextual Chunk Enrichment Pattern

# Raw chunk: "| Tier 1 | 4 hours | $500 credit |"  (Worthless alone in vector search!)

    f"[Doc: {doc.title} | Section: {chunk.section_path}]\n"
