Module 10: General AI Systems Est. 20 Mins Adaptation Architecture

System Tradeoffs: Prompting vs. RAG vs. Fine-Tuning (LoRA & DPO)

A common junior mistake is jumping straight to fine-tuning when a system needs fresh company docs—or dumping 80 pages of style examples into every prompt instead of training a compact adapter. Senior AI Engineers pick the cheapest adaptation tier that solves the exact bottleneck: Knowledge freshness demands RAG; style, latency, or proprietary syntax demands LoRA / DPO.

Primary Sources & Foundational Papers

"LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models" — Edward J. Hu et al. (Microsoft, ICLR 2022) • "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model" — Rafael Rafailov et al. (Stanford, NeurIPS 2023).

1. The Senior Decision Ladder

  1. Tier 1 — Prompting & Structured Schemas: Always start here. Zero training lag, works immediately with frontier models; sufficient when public pre-training knowledge + concise few-shot rules solve the task.
  2. Tier 2 — RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation): Required whenever facts mutate hourly/daily (commits, Jira specs, SDK changelogs) or require source attribution / ACL permissions per user.
  3. Tier 3 — LoRA / QLoRA Fine-Tuning: Freezes base weights W_0 and trains low-rank matrices A × B (e.g., rank r = 16, <1% of parameters) to teach consistent behavior, ultra-low-latency small models, or proprietary internal DSLs without massive prompt prefixes.
  4. Tier 4 — DPO Alignment: When you have chosen vs. rejected preference pairs ((x, y_w, y_l))—for example, concise surgical patches preferred over verbose rewrites—DPO optimizes behavior directly without training a separate RLHF reward model.
