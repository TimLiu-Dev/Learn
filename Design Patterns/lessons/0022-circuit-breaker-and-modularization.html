Circuit Breaker, Bulkhead & Multi-Module KMP Architecture

When PayVault's third-party ACH partner goes down and takes 30 seconds to time out, retrying every request hangs the app, drains battery, and exhausts OkHttp's connection pool—starving unrelated Balance reads! Circuit Breaker fails fast when a partner is down; Bulkhead isolates pools/dispatchers; Hexagonal Modularization enforces clean Gradle boundaries.

2027 ArchitectureCircuit BreakerBulkhead IsolationKMP Modularization

1. Circuit Breaker: Closed → Open → Half-Open

Notice how Circuit Breaker combines State Pattern (L0014) + Proxy/Decorator (L0009–L0010):

  • CLOSED: Requests flow normally. Count consecutive failures; once failures >= threshold, trip to OPEN.
  • OPEN: Reject calls immediately in <1ms (`CircuitOpenError`) without touching the network until cooldownMs elapses.
  • HALF-OPEN: After cooldown, allow 1 probe request: if it succeeds, reset to CLOSED; if it fails, trip back to OPEN!

2. Bulkhead & Clean Gradle Module Rules

Give heavy PDF/Sync jobs their own bounded `Semaphore` / dispatcher (Bulkhead) so they never starve critical user taps. And enforce Gradle module boundaries: :feature:* → :core:domain ← :core:data (feature modules never depend directly on other feature modules or raw Room/Retrofit internals).

5. 2027 Senior Android Interview Corner

Q1: "Why shouldn't `:feature:wallet` depend directly on `:feature:transfer`?"
Senior Answer: Direct feature-to-feature dependencies create tangled Gradle graphs, break parallel compilation, and risk circular dependencies. Share contracts (`TransferLauncher` or domain models) via `:core:domain` or navigation routes in `:app`.
