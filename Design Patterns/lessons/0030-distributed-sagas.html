Lesson 0030 • Part 2: Distributed Systems

Distributed Sagas & Compensating Transactions

When a cross-service transfer spans Wallet Service (Reserve Funds) → Compliance Service (AML Check) → External Bank Rail (Wire Payout), you cannot use one SQL `@Transactional` across three databases—and 2-Phase Commit (2PC) kills availability! A Saga breaks it into local transactions paired with explicit Compensating Actions if a later step fails.

Saga OrchestrationCompensationCommand PatternPivot Transaction

1. Choreography vs. Orchestration Sagas

  • Choreography: Services listen to each other's Kafka events (`FundsReserved` → Compliance checks → emits `CompliancePassed`). Great for 2–3 simple steps, turns into an untraceable pinball machine at 6+ steps.
  • Orchestration: One central TransferSagaOrchestrator (State Machine + Command!) invokes steps in order and knows the exact reverse compensation sequence (`RefundWalletReservation`). Preferred in Fintech!

2. Why Compensation Actions Must Be Idempotent

Every compensation (`releaseHold(txId)`) must be safe to run twice—if the orchestrator crashes mid-rollback and retries, releasing a \$100 hold twice must NOT add \$200 back to the user's wallet!

5. 2027 Senior Backend Interview Corner

Q1: "What is a 'Pivot Transaction' in a Fintech Saga?"
Senior Answer: The point of no return (e.g., `ExternalBankRail.executeWire()`). Steps before the pivot are compensatable (`ReserveWallet`); once the pivot succeeds, the Saga commits to forward recovery (retrying downstream notifications/ledger settlement until done) rather than rolling back!
