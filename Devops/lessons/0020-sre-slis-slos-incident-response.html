1. Mental Model Bridge: Play Console Vitals to Production SRE

Start Here

If you have watched Google Play Console's Crash-free users or ANR bad-behavior threshold, you already understand the core insight of Google SRE: 100% reliability is the wrong target. Instead, you measure user happiness with a ratio (SLI), set a target over a rolling window (SLO), and spend the remaining Error Budget on feature launches and experiments.

Android / Spring World SRE Equivalent Why It Matters in Production
Crash-free sessions % in Play Console Availability SLI (good / valid HTTP responses) Measures what users actually experience rather than raw pod CPU or JVM heap usage.
Play bad-behavior threshold (< 1.09% crashes) SLO + Error Budget (e.g., 99.9% over 30 days) Aligns product and platform teams: deploy freely while budget exists, freeze risky changes when exhausted.
Staged rollout halt at 10% Multi-window Burn-Rate Alerting Pages on-call only when error budget is burning fast enough to threaten the 30-day SLO, eliminating false alarms.
Hotfix release checklist Actionable Runbook + Incident Commander Mitigates customer pain first (rollback / feature flag) before deep debugging starts.
Retrospective after bad release Blameless Postmortem Fixes missing guardrails, tests, and automation instead of blaming the engineer who clicked merge.
Error Budget & Burn-Rate Math
With a 30-day availability SLO of 99.9%, your error budget is 1 - 0.999 = 0.001 (43.2 minutes of total downtime). A burn rate of 14.4x consumes 2% of the entire 30-day budget in just 1 hour. Pairing a long window (1h) with a short confirmation window (5m) prevents paging on brief 30-second blips while catching active outages immediately.

5 Mistakes That Burn Out On-Call Teams

  1. Paging on raw CPU or memory: High CPU on an autoscaling Spring Boot pod is healthy utilization, not an outage. Page on user-impacting SLO burn instead.
  2. Targeting 100% availability: Leaves zero room for upgrades, chaos drills, or cost efficiency.
  3. Single-window error alerts: A 5-minute spike alert triggers pages at 3 AM for transient blips that auto-recover before you open your laptop.
  4. Debugging before mitigating: Always roll back or fail over first to stop customer impact, then inspect logs and heap dumps offline.
  5. Blameful postmortems: Punishing an engineer makes teams hide mistakes; fix the missing automated check or guardrail instead.
