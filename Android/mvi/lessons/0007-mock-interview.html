Mock Interview: Say It Out Loud

You've learned the pattern. Now practise explaining it under a little time pressure, in a mixed-up order like a real interviewer would use.

Rereading lessons makes material feel familiar. Answering out loud from memory is what makes it stick, and it shows you which parts you can't yet explain. This drill shuffles questions from all six lessons, so you can't rely on the context of the lesson you just read.

How to run it

  1. Answer out loud, as if someone were in the room. Mumbling in your head doesn't count. You can record yourself on your phone if you like. It's uncomfortable, but it works.
  2. Aim for 60 seconds or less per answer. The timer is a guide, not a hard stop.
  3. Reveal the model answer and grade yourself strictly. If you got the idea but couldn't explain the mechanism (why it breaks or why it works), grade it "Shaky".
  4. Re-run only the shaky and missed cards. Then come back in 2–3 days and run all of them again. The spacing is what turns this into long-term memory.
After this lesson, you can answer the common MVI interview questions out loud in under a minute each, without notes. You also have a list of weak spots to fix before the real interview.

Take it outside the course

A drill only checks you against my model answers. Real interviewers push back, so practise with people:

  • Do a mock interview with a peer. Give them the questions above and ask them to push back with "why?" after every answer.
  • r/androiddev: search for past "MVI vs MVVM" and "one-time events" threads to see the arguments working engineers make. Post your Lesson 5 login screen and ask for critique.
  • Kotlin Slack (kotlinlang.slack.com): #android for general questions, and the Orbit channel if you choose Orbit.

Read the primary source

Before an interview, skim Google's UI layer guide once more. Interviewers usually use its vocabulary (state holder, UDF, UI state), and you'll sound fluent if you link MVI to it. Then reread the reference sheets: core loop · reducer & testing · one-off events · screen template · libraries.
