BSEE & Bench Electronics Refresher:
Voltage Rails, Pull-Ups, Termination & Probing

Even with an electrical engineering degree, years in Android app code make analog bench instincts rusty. Before plugging jumper wires into your nRF54L15-DK, STM32, ESP32, or Raspberry Pi 4, lock down the core electrical rules that prevent smoked pins and phantom bus bugs.

Mental Model Shift: Abstract Software Exceptions vs Physical Silicon Limits

In Android Kotlin, a mismatched type throws a catchable exception. On physical headers, feeding 5V UART TX into a 3.3V Raspberry Pi 4 RX pin—or wiring a 3.3V sensor to an nRF54L15-DK defaulting to 1.8V I/O—forward-biases ESD protection diodes and can silently corrupt data or permanently kill silicon.

Always verify voltage domain, common ground, pull-up stiffness, and bus termination with your Delixi multimeter before powering up!

1. Logic Levels & Day-1 Hardware Safety Traps

  • Voltage Rails First: Check VCC with your multimeter before attaching sensors. Set nRF54L15-DK I/O to 3.3V in Nordic's Board Configurator and set the CP2102 USB-UART to 3.3V before wiring to the Raspberry Pi 4.
  • Decoupling Capacitors: Every IC needs 100 nF ceramic caps right across VCC–GND to supply fast nanosecond switching currents; without them, brownout resets trigger randomly.
  • Common Ground (GND): Two boards communicating over UART, SPI, I2C, or CAN must share a ground reference (except optically/galvanically isolated buses) or voltage thresholds drift wildly.

2. Open-Drain I2C Pull-Ups & RC Rise Time

I2C chips never drive SDA or SCL HIGH—they only pull LOW through an internal MOSFET. External pull-up resistors R_p pull lines back to 3.3V, forming an RC curve with trace/breadboard capacitance C_bus (roughly t_r ≈ 0.8473 × R_p × C_bus for 30%–70% rise).

Parallel Pull-Up Rule on Shared Breadboards 
// Two modules on one I2C bus each carrying 4.7k pull-ups:
// R_total = (4.7k * 4.7k) / (4.7k + 4.7k) = 2.35 kOhm  -> Fast edges, safe!
// Sink current at 3.3V: I_sink = 3.3V / 2.35k = 1.4 mA  (Spec max = 3 mA)
// Beware: stacking 4+ modules with onboard 2.2k resistors drops R_total < 600 Ohm -> MCU fails to pull LOW!

3. Differential Pairs, 120Ω Termination & Scope Probing

  • CAN & RS485 Termination: Place a 120Ω resistor across CAN_H/CAN_L (or A/B) at each physical end of the twisted pair. Two terminated ends in parallel measure 60Ω across the unpowered bus on your multimeter.
  • Oscilloscope (DSO2512G) 10x Probe Rule: Switch probes to 10x for SPI/crystal checks (lower probe capacitance ~15 pF vs ~100 pF on 1x, avoiding rounded clock edges). Use two channels with CH1 - CH2 math to inspect differential CAN/RS485 cleanly.

int check_i2c_pullup_safe(uint32_t vcc_mv, uint32_t r_pullup_ohms) {

    if (vcc_mv > 3U * r_pullup_ohms) return 0;
    return 1;
}

Knowledge Check

Question 1 • Differential Bus Termination
With power disconnected, you place your Delixi multimeter across CAN_H and CAN_L on your two-node Blue Pill bus (each node has an onboard 120Ω resistor). What resistance confirms proper termination?
Two hundred forty ohms from series termination resistors
Sixty ohms from two parallel end termination resistors
Zero ohms because differential wires share one signal
Infinite ohms when transceivers enter recessive idle state
✓ Correct! (Option B: 60Ω from two parallel 120Ω resistors)
Both 120Ω terminators sit across the same CAN_H and CAN_L pair in parallel: 120 || 120 = 60Ω. If you add a 3rd node (like ESP32-S3), remove the middle node's 120Ω resistor!
Analysis of Distractors:
• Option A: Terminators sit in parallel across the twisted pair, not in series.
• Option C: Zero ohms indicates a dead short between CAN_H and CAN_L.
• Option D: Infinite ohms means both terminators are missing or a wire is open.
Question 2 • Oscilloscope Probing
Why should your DSO2512G oscilloscope probes be set to the 10x attenuation setting instead of 1x when measuring a 12 MHz SPI clock?
Ten-times mode removes ground loop paths from probes
Ten-times mode triples the built-in lithium battery runtime
Ten-times mode lowers probe capacitance and widens bandwidth
Ten-times mode turns single-ended wires into differential pairs
✓ Correct! (Option C: Lower capacitive loading and full probe bandwidth)
A 1x probe exposes ~100 pF of cable capacitance directly onto your SPI clock line (severely rounding edges and limiting bandwidth to ~5–10 MHz). A 10x probe divides that load down (~10–15 pF) and unlocks the scope's full bandwidth.
Analysis of Distractors:
• Options A, B, D: Ground clips remain ground-referenced on 10x probes, battery draw does not drop 3x, and probing stays single-ended unless using two channels with math subtraction.
