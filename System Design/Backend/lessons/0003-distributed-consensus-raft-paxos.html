Distributed Consensus: Raft & Paxos State Machines

Consensus is the core foundation of high-availability distributed systems. It allows a cluster of nodes to agree on a monotonically increasing log of commands despite node crashes, packet drops, and network partitions.

1. The Core Problem: Agreement Across Unreliable Networks

In an asynchronous network, physical networks experience arbitrary delays, clock drifts, and link partitions. The fundamental challenge of consensus is maintaining two safety invariants simultaneously:

  • Safety (Nothing bad happens): All non-faulty nodes agree on the exact same sequence of committed log entries. There is never more than one active leader committing writes per term (Split-Brain Prevention).
  • Liveness (Something good eventually happens): The system continues to accept writes and make forward progress as long as a strict majority of nodes ($\lfloor N/2 \rfloor + 1$) are online and connected.

2. Raft's Three Decomposed Subproblems

While Multi-Paxos is notoriously difficult to comprehend, Ongaro & Ousterhout decomposed Raft into three cleanly separated mechanisms:

Subproblem Trigger & Mechanism Safety Invariant Enforced
1. Leader Election Follower election timer expires (150ms–300ms randomized). Increments term, transitions to Candidate, votes for self, sends RequestVote RPC. Election Safety: At most one leader can be elected in a given term. Requires votes from a strict majority.
2. Log Replication Leader accepts client command, appends to local log, broadcasts AppendEntries RPC to followers. Commits entry once replicated on majority. Leader Append-Only: A leader never overwrites or truncates its own log entries; it only appends new ones.
3. Safety & Up-to-Date Log Followers reject votes if candidate's log is less up-to-date (evaluated by: higher last log term, or longer log length if terms are equal). Leader Completeness: If an entry is committed in a given term, that entry will be present in the logs of the leaders for all higher-numbered terms.
